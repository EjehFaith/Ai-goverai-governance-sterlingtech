# Portfolio Project 1: The "Is-Ought" Machine Learning Alignment
**File Name:** executive-brief.md  
**Target Enterprise:** SterlingTech Solutions (Lagos, Nigeria)  
**System Under Review:** Automated Resume Screening & Candidate Prioritization Engine  
**Frameworks Applied:** Nigeria Data Protection Act (NDPA) 2023, NIST AI Risk Management Framework (RMF)

---

## 1. The Core Problem: Hume's Law in AI Recruiting
This project governs the automated recruitment pipeline of **SterlingTech Solutions**, ensuring regulatory compliance across its engineering hiring operations.

In AI governance, **Hume’s Law** states that you cannot logically derive what *ought* to be based solely on what *is*. In automated decision-making, a dangerous engineering failure occurs when a machine learning model looks at historical data patterns (the **"Is"**) and automatically turns them into automated corporate policies (the **"Ought"**).

---

## 2. The Factual Reality vs. The Ethical Goal

### The Factual Description (The "Is")
SterlingTech’s historical training data shows that over the last decade, 85% of successful engineering hires were male and graduated from a few specific conventional universities. An unmonitored machine learning model will minimize its loss function by heavily weighting these specific features to predict candidate "success."

### The Ethical Policy (The "Ought")
Under the **Nigeria Data Protection Act (NDPA)** and the **NIST AI Risk Management Framework**, SterlingTech is legally and ethically bound to evaluate candidates based on individual technical merit, independent of legacy systemic demographic imbalances or historical bias.

---

## 3. The Objective of This Framework
As a technical policy analyst with an engineering background, the objective is to build a **Compliance Verification Gate** between SterlingTech's database and its deployment pipeline. 

Instead of treating AI ethics as an abstract set of guidelines, this framework treats policy as a set of **hard technical constraints** built directly into the software lifecycle. We acknowledge the historical data trends (The Is) but construct programmatic guardrails to enforce strict regulatory compliance (The Ought).
